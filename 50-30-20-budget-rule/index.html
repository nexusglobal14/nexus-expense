The 50/30/20 Budget Rule Explained

The 50/30/20 rule is a simple way to divide your after-tax income into three parts: 50 percent for needs, 30 percent for wants and 20 percent for savings and debt repayment. It was popularized by Elizabeth Warren and Amelia Warren Tyagi in their 2005 book All Your Worth. Its appeal is that it gives you a starting plan without complicated spreadsheets.

What counts in each bucket

  • Needs (50%) are costs you must pay to live and work: housing, utilities, basic groceries, transport to work, insurance, minimum debt payments and essential health costs.
  • Wants (30%) are things you choose: dining out, entertainment, subscriptions, travel, upgrades and hobbies.
  • Savings and debt repayment (20%) covers emergency savings, retirement or long-term investments, and any debt payments above the minimum.

Worked example

If your take-home income is 3,000 a month, the rule suggests 1,500 for needs, 900 for wants and 600 for savings or extra debt payments. The calculator above does the arithmetic for any amount.

When the rule does not fit

The percentages are a guideline, not a law. In areas with high rent or on a lower income, needs may take 60 to 70 percent of your pay. In that case, protect even a small savings amount and look for ways to raise income or reduce your largest fixed cost. If you have little debt and a high income, you might save more than 20 percent. Adjust the split to match your situation.

How to apply it in practice

  1. Work out your monthly after-tax income.
  2. List last month's spending and label each item as a need or a want.
  3. Compare your totals with the 50/30/20 targets.
  4. Move savings out of your account early in the month, so you do not spend it by accident.
  5. Track your spending in the expense tracker and review monthly.

Frequently asked questions

Is the 50/30/20 rule based on gross or net income?

It is normally applied to after-tax (take-home) income.

Where does debt repayment go?

Minimum payments count as needs. Any extra payment beyond the minimum counts in the 20 percent savings and debt bucket.

What if I cannot save 20 percent?

Start with what you can, even 5 percent, and increase it as your income grows or your costs fall.

Are subscriptions a need or a want?

Usually a want, unless the service is required for work or daily life.
